I’m tired of being told to “lower the temperature” by the same culture that keeps lighting matches.

When you spend years telling people their political opponents are monsters, you don’t get to act surprised when somebody eventually decides monsters don’t deserve rights, safety, or life. You can call it "just rhetoric" all you want. Rhetoric is how normal people get permission to do disgusting things.

I’m writing this in the shadow of two stories that hit like a punch. One is the reported killing of a young Ukrainian refugee, Iryna Zarutska, on public transit. The other is reports swirling about an attack on Charlie Kirk during a campus event. I’m not going to pretend I can personally verify every detail of what’s circulating online, and I’m not interested in turning tragedy into fan fiction. But I am interested in the pattern, because the pattern is real.
<!--more-->

The pattern is this: intimidation has become normal, and everybody’s pretending it’s just "politics". It’s not. When people get punished for speaking, when they lose jobs for a post, when they get swarmed by a digital mob, when events require security like we’re living in a failed state, that’s not healthy democracy. That’s a society sliding into fear.

And yes, I’m going to say the quiet part out loud. The modern left, as a cultural force, plays footsie with dehumanization. The easy labels are the point. "Nazi", "Fascist", "Threat", and so on. Once you’ve slapped that on someone, you don’t have to argue with them. You just have to remove them. You can dress it up as virtue all you want. The psychology is the same.

No, I’m not saying every progressive is violent. I’m saying the cultural incentives reward cruelty, reward censorship, reward the idea that certain people are beyond the protection of normal rules. You see it in the way speech gets framed as "harm", as if words are violence but actual violence is just "context". You see it in the way institutions cower. Tech platforms throttle dissent and call it "safety". Universities can’t even host a speaker without turning it into a moral panic. That’s not enlightenment, but cowardice with a mission statement.

If you want fewer tragedies, the fix isn’t more moral lectures. It’s a public culture that treats violence and intimidation as unforgivable, not as a cute side effect of "passion". It’s a justice system that prioritizes public safety over ideological fashion. It’s adults in charge of schools who remember education is supposed to produce thinkers, not activists.

And for the right, the answer is not revenge. I’m not interested in "fighting fire with fire". That’s how you burn the house down and then brag you won. The answer is to stop retreating. Stop whispering like you’re ashamed of believing in free speech, law and order, and basic reality.

Show up. Speak clearly. Support people who take the heat. Push back on the institutional bullying. Build parallel spaces when you have to. Vote like it matters, because it does. And for God’s sake, don’t let the people who smeared you as evil for years lecture you about civility the moment they lose control of the room.

Words don’t fail on their own. They fail when cowards decide intimidation is easier than debate. Don’t reward the cowards.
