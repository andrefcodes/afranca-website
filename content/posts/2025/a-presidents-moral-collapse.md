Lula calling drug traffickers "victims" is the kind of thing that makes me wonder if the people running Brazil are living on the same planet as the rest of us.
<!--more-->

He said it while traveling abroad, in Jakarta, with the easy confidence of a man who's never had to hear gunfire outside his window or watch an entire neighborhood get carved up by armed morons with radios and rifles. Traffickers aren't victims. They're the guys who turn streets into checkpoints, recruit kids, terrorize families, and buy politicians with the same cash they squeeze out of addicts and extortion.

And yeah, I know the little cleanup line that comes after. He tried to walk it back as a "poorly placed phrase". Sure. Politicians always "misspeak" when the backlash hits, and never when they're in friendly rooms where everyone nods along. If you can look at the drug trade in Brazil and your first instinct is to humanize the people running it, you're already lost.

This isn't a debate about whether drug users exist, or whether addiction is tragic, or whether the U.S. has made dumb choices in anti-drug policy. Of course it has. You can argue about harm reduction until your throat is sore. None of that requires pretending the violent networks controlling territory in Brazil are somehow the real casualties of consumer demand.

And no, I'm not doing the lazy "crime is just poverty" bedtime story. Poverty is real. Lack of opportunity is real. But plenty of poor Brazilians don't join violent organizations. Choice exists. The people who act like it doesn't are usually the ones protected from the consequences.

Meanwhile, regular people live inside the consequences.

I'm tired of leaders who speak like crime is an abstract sociology lecture. It's not. It's control of territory. It's intimidation.

If Lula's actual argument was "the U.S. should focus more on reducing demand", fine, say that. If he wants to criticize Trump-era anti-narcotics talk and macho rhetoric, go ahead. But don't smuggle in this disgusting premise that the trafficker is the one being harmed by the existence of users. That's like calling the guy running a kidnapping ring a victim of people who like ransom movies.

Lula can keep his "poorly placed phrases". The reality is still there, and it doesn't give a damn about his rhetoric.
