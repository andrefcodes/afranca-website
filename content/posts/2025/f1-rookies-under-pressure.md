Formula 1 loves selling the romance of talent, but it runs on impatience.

For rookies it’s a pressure cooker where every mistake gets replayed, analyzed, meme’d, and weaponized. This year, alongside the Hamilton-to-Ferrari noise and the McLaren hype, we’ve got a fresh batch of kids trying to survive the big leagues: Andrea Kimi Antonelli, Liam Lawson, Oliver Bearman, Gabriel Bortoleto, Jack Doohan, and Isack Hadjar.
<!--more-->

Let me start with Bortoleto, because I’m biased and I don’t care. The kid is in what looks like the worst car on the grid and still manages to make you notice him.

He looks composed, he fights smart, and he’s extracting more from that car than it has any right to give.

Red Bull is doing what Red Bull always does, that is, chewing through young drivers like it’s a feature.

Lawson got two bad races and a sprint, and that was enough for the machine to decide he’s done, demotion, swap, next. I’m not saying he was lighting it up. I’m saying two weekends is a joke of a sample size when you’re adapting to a car and a team that expect perfection as the entry fee.

People defend this by saying Red Bull gives kids a shot. Sure. They also turn that shot into a firing squad. It’s a system optimized for short-term results, not development. If you’re not immediately useful, you’re disposable.

Antonelli is the opposite story. You can see the raw speed. You can also see the rookie mistakes. That’s normal. That’s what “learning” looks like.

If Mercedes wants him long-term, they’ll need patience and the ability to not panic when he has a bad Sunday.

Bearman has Ferrari expectations hanging over him like a storm cloud. Everything becomes a “test” for whether he deserves a future seat. He showed promise before. Now he has to turn promise into repeatable weekends, and F1 is not generous with time.

Doohan and Hadjar are in that awkward middle where you’re not awful, but you’re also not instantly “the story”. Doohan has the name, the expectations, and the constant threat of being replaced. Push too hard and you look reckless. Don’t push hard enough and you look slow.

Hadjar feels more methodical. Less headline-chasing, more accumulation. That kind of rookie can surprise you if the team doesn’t get bored.

What’s obvious watching all of them is that environment matters as much as talent. Give a rookie some breathing room and they can grow. Put them under a microscope with a trap door under the seat and they’ll drive like they’re auditioning for their life.

F1 loves pretending it’s a pure meritocracy. It’s not. It’s talent plus timing plus politics plus whether your team gives you a chance to be bad while you learn.

Talent is the ticket in the door. After that, it’s survival.
