This post is RSS-only because I don’t trust the current climate in Brazil. Call it paranoia if you want. I call it pattern recognition. People who criticize powerful institutions get dragged, doxxed, investigated, and turned into examples. I’m not volunteering to become content for someone else’s political theater.

<!--more-->

Anyway, the 2026 presidential race. Polls come and go, and Brazil is a country where "sure thing" candidates have a way of turning into "how did that happen" candidates overnight. Still, the basic shape of it is what you’d expect: Lula is sitting there like the default setting, and the opposition side is a messy pile of brands, egos, and people auditioning for attention.

Lula, to me, is the symbol of everything that keeps Brazil stuck. Not because he’s the only problem, but because he’s the clearest proof the country never really deals with its own political rot. His style is old-school machine politics. i.e. spend, expand, promise, and keep the base happy with benefits while the bill gets shoved to the future. People talk like he’s a working-class saint. Give me a break. I’ve watched this movie before, and the ending is always more inflation risk, fiscal gimmicks, and a public sector that grows like a weed.

And the moral part matters too. The PT (workers party) era isn’t remembered for clean governance. Mensalão and Petrolão (two of the biggest corruption scandals) weren’t hallucinations. If your response to that is "yeah but other people stole too", congrats, you’ve basically described why Brazil never improves. Lowering the bar doesn’t make you righteous. It just makes you comfortable.

On the other side, Flávio Bolsonaro is not some heroic antidote. I don’t do personality cults, and I especially don’t do dynasty politics. If your whole pitch is your last name and your tribe’s rage, you don’t have a program. You have a marketing strategy. And Brazil has already had enough “family brand” politics to last a century.

Romeu Zema, from what I’ve seen, plays the cautious game too much. He tries to thread the needle between camps that hate each other, and you can’t lead a country like Brazil with permanent triangulation. When the country’s problems include organized crime controlling territory, institutions losing legitimacy, and a state that’s expensive and ineffective, "I’ll try not to offend anyone" is not leadership.

Ronaldo Caiado feels like more of the same establishment DNA. If you’ve been watching Brazilian politics for longer than five minutes, you know the type: well-connected, seasoned, good at surviving, and not exactly built to break the cycle.

Renan Santos is the one I see actually trying to put proposals on the table instead of vibes. I’m not saying every idea is perfect, but at least it’s a real attempt at reform instead of nostalgia or tribal revenge. And of course he gets treated like background noise, because Brazil’s political ecosystem rewards loudness and punishes seriousness.

Here’s my bottom line. Brazil can’t keep choosing between a left that treats security like a slogan and a right that keeps falling for opportunists with famous surnames. Ordinary people pay for this. They pay when crime expands, when inflation eats their paycheck, when the state fails at basics and then demands more money anyway.

I’m writing this RSS-only because I’m not interested in feeding the outrage machine. But I’m also not going to pretend the stakes are polite. If Brazil sleepwalks into another term of the same old political addiction, don’t act surprised when the country keeps getting exactly what it tolerates.

This country doesn’t need another savior. It needs voters who stop falling for bullshit.
